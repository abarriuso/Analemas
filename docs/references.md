> **Note on rigour:** outreach and historical sources are kept only for the historical or documentary claims they actually support. ## Notes on specific references

- **Heller et al. (2011):** the correct DOI is `10.1051/0004-6361/201015809` (an earlier list gave `...15817`, which is wrong). The *Astronomy & Astrophysics* record confirms volume 528, article A27 and that DOI.
- **Sawyer (1994):** *Bulletin of the British Sundial Society*, **6(2)**, June 1994, pp. **2–6**, as confirmed by the British Sundial Society archive; part II appeared in February 1995, vol. 7(1), pp. 39–44.
- **Standish et al. (1992):** pages **279–323** of chapter 5.
- **Bricker & Bricker (2011):** volume 265 of the *Memoirs of the American Philosophical Society*.
- **Williams:** the NASA page should not be labelled generically as "2024". The *Planetary Fact Sheet* currently names David R. Williams as author/curator and shows an update date of **18 March 2025**; the fact sheet notes page shows **6 May 2024**.
- Web sources that change over time should be cited with their update date when available; APA 7 treats updated pages and retrieval dates differently from stable pages.

## Recommended source hierarchy

For the project's scientific text:

1. **Computing the equation of time:** Hughes et al. (1989), Meeus (1998) and USNO/HMNAO.
2. **Orbital elements and ephemerides:** Standish et al. (1992), Seidelmann (1992) and JPL/NASA resources.
3. **Planetary parameters:** NASA NSSDCA/Williams, giving the specific update date. NASA's own notes warn that the fact sheet values are not a single "official" set and may change with new research.
4. **Exoplanets and insolation:** Dobrovolskis (2013) and Heller et al. (2011).
5. **History, photography and outreach:** Aveni, Bricker & Bricker, di Cicco, Sawyer, Waugh, Lakdawalla and Urschel.
